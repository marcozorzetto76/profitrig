The three costs every pressure washing business forgets

They are not exotic. They are not hard to calculate. They are just invisible on a bank statement, which is why a season that felt busy ends with less money than it should.

Ask an operator what an hour costs them and you usually get a version of this: fuel, chemicals, and a rough allowance for wear. Maybe labour if there is a crew.

That answer is not wrong. It is just missing about a third of the real number. Published industry guidance puts the typical underestimate at 35–40%, and the gap comes from three places.

1. Unbillable hours

You do not get paid for driving to the job. Or quoting the job you did not win. Or loading the trailer, chasing an invoice, answering the phone at 8pm, or the Saturday you spent servicing a pump.

Those hours are real. They come out of the same day as the billable ones. If you bill six hours in a ten-hour day, every cost you carry has to be recovered across six hours, not ten — which means your true cost per billable hour is roughly 1.6× what you thought.

The test: take last week. Count the hours you invoiced. Count the hours you worked. Divide the second by the first. That ratio is the multiplier sitting on top of every cost you have.

2. Equipment replacement

A surface cleaner does not fail gradually in your accounts. It works, and then one Tuesday it does not, and $600 leaves your bank in an afternoon. Because that is how it feels, most operators treat it as bad luck rather than as a cost.

It is a cost, and it is a predictable one. A machine with a three-year life is charging you a third of its price every year whether you set the money aside or not. The only question is whether it appears in your rate or as a surprise.

Spread across, say, 900 billable hours a year, that is $1.56 per billable hour that has to be in your price. Small on its own. Not small when it is one of three.

3. What your vehicle actually costs per mile

This is the one that is most often reduced to fuel, and fuel is the smallest part of it.

A work truck costs you fuel, insurance, tax, servicing, tyres, repairs, and depreciation — and depreciation is usually the largest single line even though no money visibly leaves your account. Add them, divide by annual mileage, and the honest figure typically lands well above what most people assume.

$0.62 / mile
A representative all-in figure once depreciation, insurance, servicing and tyres are included alongside fuel. On a 24-mile round trip that is $14.88 — before you have cleaned anything.

Put together

None of the three is dramatic alone. Together they are usually the entire gap between the hourly rate you think you earn and the one you actually do — and they are the reason a season can feel relentless and still end thin.

The arithmetic is not difficult. It is just tedious, and it has to be redone every time fuel moves or you replace a machine. That is the whole reason a spreadsheet exists.
