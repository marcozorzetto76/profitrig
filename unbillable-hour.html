What an unbillable hour actually costs you

Every service business has them. Very few price for them. Here is the arithmetic, in full.

An unbillable hour is any hour you work that nobody pays for. Driving between jobs. Quoting work you did not win. Loading and unloading. Cleaning the van. Chasing an invoice. Answering the phone in the evening. Servicing your own kit on a Saturday.

Everyone knows these hours exist. The mistake is treating them as an unfortunate side-effect instead of as what they are: a cost that has to be recovered from the hours that do get paid.

The arithmetic

Say your business costs $600 a day to run — your own wage, the vehicle, insurance, equipment allowance, overheads, chemicals. That number does not care how many hours you invoice. It is the same on a full day and an empty one.

Same effort. Same costs. The only variable is how much of the day someone paid for — and the true cost per billable hour more than doubles between the first row and the last.

The multiplier sitting on every cost you have, if you bill six hours in a ten-hour day. Quote as if it were 1.0 and you are funding the difference yourself.

Why it stays invisible

Because there is no invoice for it, and no line on a bank statement. The money does not leave in an identifiable lump — it simply never arrives. A cost that shows up as absence rather than as an expense is one the brain refuses to file as a cost at all.

It is also why the classic advice — "charge $75 an hour, that's the going rate" — can be perfectly accurate and still leave you working for very little. The going rate is what the market pays per billable hour. It says nothing about how many billable hours you actually get out of a day.

Run this once, this week. Total hours worked, divided by hours invoiced. If the answer is 1.6, then a job you quoted at four hours needs to carry six hours forty minutes of your costs. Quote it as four and you have donated the rest.

What to do about it

Two levers, and only two.

Price for the real ratio. Use cost per billable hour, not cost per worked hour, as the floor under every quote. This does not require charging more than the market — it requires knowing when a job is below your floor before you send the number, not after.

Move the ratio. Tighter routing so drive time falls. Quoting by phone or photo where you reasonably can, instead of driving out. Grouping jobs by area on the same day. Every unbillable hour you remove goes straight to the bottom line, because your costs were being paid either way.
